# Marine Underwriting Operations Manual

This manual describes how the marine underwriting team at RAGBench Mutual handles a broker submission from the moment it arrives to the moment a quote is bound or declined. It assumes the team uses HarborShield AI for first-pass review. The manual is operational: it says who does what, in what order, and within what time.

## 1. Purpose and scope

The manual covers ocean cargo submissions received through the Broker Portal or by email. It does not cover hull and machinery business, which follows a separate manual, nor claims, which are covered by the Claims Handling Standards.

The goals of the process are to give brokers a fast and predictable answer, to keep risk selection consistent between underwriters, and to keep a complete, auditable record of why each decision was made. Speed matters, but never at the expense of the second and third goals.

## 2. Roles

**Underwriter.** Owns a submission from first review to quote. Applies the referral rules in this manual and binds within the authority limit described later.

**Senior underwriter.** Reviews referrals, mentors underwriters, and binds within a higher authority limit.

**Head of Marine Underwriting.** Decides the largest and most unusual risks and owns the referral rules themselves.

**Compliance officer.** Handles every submission that has a possible sanctions match. Underwriters never clear a sanctions match on their own.

**Intake coordinator.** Watches the intake queue, bounces incomplete packets, and makes sure nothing sits on a file beyond the service target.

## 3. Intake

### 3.1 Required fields

A submission packet is complete, or "clean", when it contains all nine required intake fields:

1. named insured
2. broker
3. vessel name
4. vessel IMO number
5. voyage origin port
6. voyage destination port
7. commodity
8. declared cargo value
9. policy period

HarborShield AI extracts these fields from the packet and marks each one as present, missing, or low confidence. A packet with any missing field is a "dirty packet" and is handled as described in section 3.3.

### 3.2 Channels

Submissions arrive through the Broker Portal, which accepts PDF, DOCX, TXT, and email-body submissions, or by email to the shared submissions mailbox. The intake coordinator uploads emailed packets into the Broker Portal so that every submission has the same audit trail. Emailed packets are logged within 30 minutes of arrival during business hours.

### 3.3 Bouncing a packet

If a required field is missing, the intake coordinator bounces the packet back to the broker. A bounce must be sent within 1 business day of the packet arriving, and it must list every missing field at once; sending a second bounce for a field that could have been listed in the first is treated as a process failure. The bounce message uses the standard template, which links to the Broker Portal FAQ.

When a packet has low-confidence fields but no missing ones, do not bounce it. Verify the low-confidence fields by hand and continue.

## 4. First-pass review with HarborShield AI

### 4.1 What the tool does

HarborShield AI extracts the vessel route, commodity, port, and sanctions information from the broker packet and produces a first-pass risk memo. The memo is a draft for the underwriter. It is advisory and is never a final coverage or pricing decision.

### 4.2 Turnaround

For a clean packet the first-pass risk memo must be in the underwriter's hands within 4 business hours of the packet arriving. The memo clock starts when the packet is complete, not when the first incomplete version arrived.

### 4.3 Reading the confidence score

Every memo shows an overall confidence score between 0 and 1. If the score is below 0.65, the underwriter must re-read the route and the commodity description in the original packet rather than relying on the memo. A score at or above 0.65 still requires the underwriter to read the memo critically, but the route re-read is not mandatory.

### 4.4 Known limitations

Two limitations matter in daily work. First, the tool can miss a route that is described only in an image embedded in the packet; if the route page is a scanned picture, check it by hand. Second, flag-state explanations are brief; when the flag state of the vessel matters to the decision, read the source document and do not rely on the one-line summary.

## 5. The risk memo

Every memo, whether produced by the tool or written by hand, follows the same six-part structure so that reviewers know where to look:

1. **Summary of the risk:** who, what, where, how much.
2. **Route and exposures:** ports, transit areas, and any exposure to listed areas.
3. **Commodity assessment:** the nature of the cargo, packaging, and any special handling needs.
4. **Vessel assessment:** age, flag state, classification society, and history.
5. **Sanctions and compliance screening:** results and any escalation taken.
6. **Recommendation:** accept, accept with conditions, refer, or decline, with the reasons.

The recommendation section is written by the underwriter, not by the tool. Underwriters may edit any section, but the original tool output is preserved in the audit trail.

## 6. Authority and referrals

### 6.1 Underwriter authority

An underwriter may bind a cargo risk whose declared cargo value is up to $5 million, provided that none of the mandatory referral triggers in section 6.4 applies.

### 6.2 Senior underwriter authority

A senior underwriter may bind a cargo risk whose declared cargo value is up to $25 million. Risks between $5 million and $25 million must be referred to a senior underwriter before any quote is released.

### 6.3 Head of Marine Underwriting authority

Risks with a declared cargo value above $25 million must be referred to the Head of Marine Underwriting. Before the Head decides, the underwriter must confirm that reinsurance capacity is available for the risk; the Head will not release a quote without that confirmation.

### 6.4 Mandatory referral triggers

Regardless of cargo value, the following triggers require referral to a senior underwriter:

- The vessel is more than 25 years old.
- The vessel is not classed by a recognized classification society.
- The voyage transits a listed area for war risk.
- The commodity is on the restricted commodity list described in section 8.
- The HarborShield AI confidence score is below 0.65 and the underwriter could not resolve the uncertainty from the source documents.

If more than one trigger applies, one referral covers all of them, but the referral note must list every trigger.

## 7. Sanctions and compliance

### 7.1 Screening

HarborShield AI screens the vessel, the named insured, the broker, and the ports against the sanctions list snapshot. The snapshot is refreshed on a schedule that depends on the customer's plan. If the snapshot is stale, the tool reports error code HS-4202 and the submission cannot be cleared until a fresh snapshot is available.

### 7.2 Possible matches

If the screening reports a possible match, the underwriter must stop work on the submission and escalate to a Compliance officer within 2 hours. The underwriter does not contact the broker about the match, does not release a quote, and does not bind. Compliance decides whether the match is a true hit, and records the decision in the audit trail.

### 7.3 Confirmed matches

For a confirmed match the submission is declined and Compliance decides whether any regulatory report must be filed. Reporting deadlines are handled by Compliance, not by the underwriting team.

### 7.4 Language in communications

Underwriters must not describe the tool's output to a broker as a final decision. The standard wording is that the output is a first-pass review that remains subject to underwriter review.

## 8. Restricted commodities and declinatures

### 8.1 Restricted commodity list

The following commodities always trigger a senior underwriter referral: lithium batteries in bulk, hazardous chemicals of any class other than class 1, refrigerated pharmaceuticals, and live animals.

### 8.2 Commodities that are declined

Class 1 explosives are declined without exception. Cargo declared as cash or bullion is also declined; it belongs with the specie team.

### 8.3 Writing a decline

A decline must give the broker a clear reason and must be sent within 1 business day of the decision. Do not cite the tool as the reason for a decline; the reason is always the underwriting criteria.

## 9. Quoting and binding

### 9.1 Quote validity

A quote is valid for 30 days from the date it is issued. After that the broker must request a refreshed quote, and the underwriter must re-screen the submission for sanctions because the list may have changed.

### 9.2 Conditions

Conditions attached to a quote, such as required survey reports or special packing, must be written in the quote itself and repeated on the binder. Verbal conditions are not valid.

### 9.3 Binding

Before binding, the underwriter checks that the referral rules have been followed, that the sanctions screening is current, and that the audit trail contains the memo and the recommendation. A bind without these is reversed and reviewed.

## 10. Quality assurance

### 10.1 Peer review sampling

Each month, 10 percent of first-pass memos are sampled for peer review by a senior underwriter who did not work on the submission. The reviewer scores accuracy of extraction, quality of the recommendation, and completeness of the audit trail.

### 10.2 Error-rate target

The target is an extraction error rate of below 2 percent in the monthly sample. If the rate is above the target for two months in a row, the team lead opens an improvement plan and the tool's results are discussed with the product team.

### 10.3 Feedback to the product team

Systematic extraction errors are reported to the HarborShield AI product lead through the feedback form. Include the packet reference, the field that was wrong, and what the correct value was. Do not include customer data in the free-text field.

## 11. Exceptions and escalation

### 11.1 Requesting an exception

If a submission needs an exception to this manual, the underwriter writes up the reason and sends it to the Head of Marine Underwriting. The Head decides within 1 business day. Exceptions are recorded in the audit trail and reviewed quarterly.

### 11.2 Service failures

If HarborShield AI is unavailable, the team falls back to manual review using the six-part memo structure. The intake coordinator tells brokers that turnaround may be longer and logs each affected submission so that it can be checked once the tool is back.

### 11.3 Broker escalations

A broker who is unhappy with the handling of a submission can escalate to the senior underwriter on duty. The senior underwriter replies within 4 business hours and, if needed, involves the Head of Marine Underwriting.

## 13. Appendix A: worked examples

### 13.1 A clean packet under the underwriter limit

A broker sends a packet for 40 containers of ceramic tiles travelling between two well-known ports. All nine required fields are present, the vessel is 11 years old and classed by a recognized society, the route does not transit a listed area, and the declared cargo value is $2.4 million. HarborShield AI produces a memo with a confidence score of 0.91 and no sanctions flags. The underwriter reads the memo, confirms the commodity description, and quotes within the underwriter authority. No referral is needed.

### 13.2 A packet that needs a senior underwriter

A packet declares $12 million of machinery. The vessel is 14 years old and there are no other triggers, but the value sits above the underwriter limit of $5 million and below the senior underwriter limit of $25 million. The underwriter refers it to a senior underwriter before releasing any quote. The referral note records the value as the only trigger.

### 13.3 A packet with multiple triggers

A packet declares $3 million of refrigerated pharmaceuticals on a 28-year-old vessel. The value alone would be within the underwriter limit, but there are two triggers: the commodity is on the restricted list and the vessel is older than 25 years. The underwriter sends one referral that lists both triggers. The senior underwriter may add conditions, such as a temperature-monitoring requirement and a survey report, both of which must be written into the quote.

### 13.4 A possible sanctions match

During screening HarborShield AI reports a possible match between a named insured and a listed entity. The underwriter stops work, does not reply to the broker about the match, and escalates to a Compliance officer within 2 hours. Compliance reviews the match, concludes that it is a false positive caused by a similar name, and records the decision. Only then does the underwriter resume work. The memo's sanctions section records the escalation, the time it was made, and the outcome.

### 13.5 A low-confidence route

A scanned packet yields a memo with a confidence score of 0.52 because the route page was a picture of a handwritten voyage plan. The score is below 0.65, so the underwriter re-reads the original route page, corrects the route by hand, and notes the correction in the audit trail. Because the underwriter could resolve the uncertainty from the source document, no referral is triggered.

## 15. Appendix C: the audit trail

The audit trail for a submission is a single record, kept for as long as the retention policy requires, that must allow a reviewer who was not involved to understand the decision. It contains the original packet, the tool output exactly as produced, every edit to the memo with the editor and time, the sanctions screening result, any referral and its outcome, the final recommendation, and the quote or decline that was sent.

Audit trails are sampled for peer review as described in section 10. A missing element is scored as a defect even if the decision itself was right. Underwriters must never edit or delete entries in the audit trail; corrections are added as new entries that refer to the old one.

## 16. Appendix D: working with brokers

Brokers are our customers' partners and deserve clear, consistent communication. Acknowledge every submission on the day it arrives. Give a realistic date for the quote, and keep to it. If you must decline, explain the reason in terms the broker can act on, for example that the vessel age is outside appetite, and do not leave the broker guessing.

Avoid informal promises. A statement such as "this will be fine" may be read as an undertaking. Conditions, limits, and prices are valid only when written in the quote. If a broker presses for a verbal indication before the memo is complete, offer a timeline instead of an indication.

When a broker reports a mistake in the tool's extraction, thank them, correct the record, and report the error through the feedback form. Brokers who find errors are helping us improve the process, and they should hear that their feedback was used.
