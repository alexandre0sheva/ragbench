# Platform Operations Runbook

This runbook is for the engineers who keep the Broker Portal, the document extraction service, and the three RAGBench Mutual products running. It covers day-to-day operations, deployments, scaling, incident response, and disaster recovery. It is maintained by the reliability engineering team and reviewed after every Severity 1 incident.

## 1. Architecture at a glance

The platform has four layers. The edge layer terminates customer connections and enforces rate limits. The Broker Portal layer handles authentication, uploads, and the public API. The processing layer runs the document extraction workers and the model-serving services behind HarborShield AI, ClaimPilot, and Aurora Risk Suite. The data layer holds the primary database, its replicas, and encrypted object storage for uploaded documents.

Uploads flow from the edge to the Broker Portal, which stores the file and places a message on the extraction queue. Extraction workers take messages from the queue, extract text and structure, and pass the result to the product services. Because the queue sits between the portal and the workers, a slow worker pool shows up first as a growing queue, long before customers see errors. That is why queue depth is the most important health signal in this document.

## 2. Severity levels

**Severity 1.** A broad outage, or a problem that affects data integrity, for multiple customers. Acknowledge within 30 minutes, appoint an incident commander within 45 minutes.

**Severity 2.** A significant degradation for one customer or a minor degradation for many. Acknowledge within 4 business hours.

**Severity 3.** A defect with a workaround. Handled in the normal ticket flow.

The severity is set by whoever receives the first page and can be raised at any time by the incident commander. When in doubt, pick the higher severity; it is easy to downgrade.

## 3. On-call

### 3.1 Rotation

On-call rotates weekly and the handoff is every Monday at 09:00 UTC. The outgoing engineer writes a handoff note covering open incidents, flaky alerts, and anything scheduled for the week. The incoming engineer acknowledges the note in the reliability channel before the handoff is considered complete.

### 3.2 Paging policy

A page must mean that a human needs to act now. Alerts that cannot justify waking someone up are configured to create a ticket instead. The on-call engineer is expected to acknowledge a page within 15 minutes.

### 3.3 Escalation path

If the on-call engineer does not acknowledge a page within 15 minutes, the page goes to the secondary on-call. If the secondary also does not acknowledge within 15 minutes, it goes to the reliability engineering lead.

## 4. The extraction worker pool

### 4.1 Sizing

The extraction worker pool runs with a baseline of 10 workers. The baseline was raised from 4 workers to 10 after the API latency incident on 2024-02-14, in which HarborShield AI requests queued behind a slow document extraction worker. The pool can scale out to a maximum of 24 workers.

### 4.2 Autoscaling rules

The autoscaler reads the depth of the extraction queue every 30 seconds and acts as follows:

- **Scale out:** if the queue depth stays above 200 messages for 3 consecutive minutes, add 4 workers, up to the maximum.
- **Scale in:** if the queue depth stays below 50 messages for 15 consecutive minutes, remove 2 workers, down to the baseline.
- **Cooldown:** after any scaling action, wait 5 minutes before the next one.

The scale-in rule is deliberately slow. Removing workers too quickly caused oscillation in the first version of the autoscaler, where the pool repeatedly shrank just before the next wave of uploads arrived.

### 4.3 Queue-depth alerts

Two alerts watch the extraction queue. A warning ticket is created if the queue depth is above 200 for more than 10 minutes. A page is sent if the queue depth is above 500 messages for 5 minutes. A page for queue depth should prompt the on-call engineer to check whether the pool has already hit its maximum of 24 workers; if it has, the next step is to look for a stuck worker rather than add capacity.

### 4.4 Stuck workers

A worker is considered stuck if it has held the same message for more than 10 minutes. The supervisor restarts stuck workers automatically, and the message is returned to the queue. If the same message causes three worker restarts, it is moved to the dead-letter queue and a ticket is created so that a human can look at the document.

## 5. Deployments

### 5.1 Deployment windows

Production deployments happen on Tuesdays and Thursdays between 10:00 and 15:00 UTC. There are no deployments on Fridays or at weekends, except for emergency fixes approved by the incident commander or the reliability lead.

### 5.2 Release checklist

Before deploying, confirm that the build passed all tests, that the change has a rollback plan, that the migration (if any) is backward compatible with the previous version, and that the on-call engineer knows about the deployment. After deploying, watch the error rate and the queue depth for at least 30 minutes.

### 5.3 Canary and rollout

Changes roll out first to a canary group serving 5 percent of traffic. If the error rate and latency stay within bounds for 20 minutes, the rollout proceeds to 25 percent, then 100 percent. The canary automatically rolls back if the error rate doubles.

### 5.4 Change freezes

There is a change freeze over the year-end period. The 2024 freeze starts on 2024-12-16 and ends on 2025-01-03. During a freeze only emergency fixes are deployed, and every emergency fix needs written approval from the reliability lead.

### 5.5 Rollbacks

Any engineer may roll back a deployment that they believe is causing customer impact, without asking permission first. The rollback is reported in the reliability channel immediately, and the cause is investigated after service is restored.

## 6. Certificates and secrets

### 6.1 Certificate expiry alerts

Certificate expiry alerts fire 45 days, 14 days, and 3 days before expiry. The 45-day alert was added after the incident on 2024-04-12, in which an expired internal certificate on the extraction ingress stopped uploads. Earlier, the only alert fired 7 days before expiry.

### 6.2 Rotating secrets

Service tokens are rotated every 90 days. Rotation is automated, but the engineer who owns the service must confirm that the new token is working before the old one is revoked. Revoking a token without confirming the new one is a common cause of self-inflicted outages.

### 6.3 Emergency revocation

If a token may have been exposed, revoke it immediately and rotate afterward. Accept a short disruption rather than leave an exposed token active. Tell the security team at once.

## 7. Incident response

### 7.1 Declaring an incident

Anyone can declare an incident. Open the incident channel, state the symptoms, and page the on-call engineer. The first responder sets an initial severity and starts a timeline in the incident document.

### 7.2 Roles

The **incident commander** coordinates the response and makes decisions. The **operations lead** drives technical investigation and mitigation. The **communications lead** posts updates to customers and keeps the status page current. For a Severity 1 incident all three roles are filled by different people.

### 7.3 Status page cadence

For a Severity 1 incident the first status page update must be posted within 15 minutes of declaring the incident. After that, updates are posted at least every 30 minutes until the incident is resolved. Updates say what customers are experiencing, what the team is doing, and when the next update will come, even when there is nothing new.

### 7.4 Resolving and closing

An incident is resolved when customer impact has ended. It is closed when the post-incident report is published. The incident owner writes the report within 5 business days of resolution. Reports are blameless: they describe what happened and how the system allowed it, not who was at fault.

### 7.5 Post-incident report contents

Each report states the start time and end time in UTC, the duration, the customers affected, the cause, the fix, and the corrective actions with owners and due dates. Corrective actions are tracked in the reliability board and reviewed monthly.

## 8. Database operations

### 8.1 Topology

The primary database has two replicas in the primary region and one replica in the secondary region. Reads for dashboards go to the replicas; writes go only to the primary.

### 8.2 Replica lag

An alert fires if replica lag exceeds 30 seconds. The threshold was tightened from 120 seconds after the incident on 2024-09-19, where a failover promoted a replica that had fallen far behind. A replica whose lag is above the threshold must never be promoted without the incident commander's explicit approval.

### 8.3 Failover

Automatic failover promotes the healthiest replica in the primary region. Failover to the secondary region is always manual and requires the incident commander's approval, because it carries a higher risk of data loss.

### 8.4 Migrations

Schema migrations must be backward compatible with the previous application version so that a rollback does not break the database. Large migrations run outside the deployment windows with the reliability lead's approval.

## 9. Backups and disaster recovery

### 9.1 Backup schedule

A full backup of the primary database runs nightly at 02:00 UTC and is retained for 35 days. Transaction logs are shipped continuously, which allows point-in-time recovery.

### 9.2 Recovery objectives

The recovery point objective is 15 minutes: after a disaster, we may lose at most the last 15 minutes of data. The recovery time objective is 4 hours: service must be restored within 4 hours of declaring a disaster.

### 9.3 Restore tests

Restore tests run quarterly. The team restores the latest backup into an isolated environment, checks data integrity, and measures how long the restore takes. A restore that takes more than 3 hours is treated as a finding, because it leaves too little room inside the recovery time objective.

### 9.4 Disaster declaration

A disaster is declared by the incident commander when the primary region cannot serve traffic and cannot be expected to recover inside the recovery time objective. Declaring a disaster triggers the regional failover procedure and notifies the executive on call.

## 10. Observability

### 10.1 Logs

Application logs are kept searchable for 30 days and archived for 13 months. Logs must never contain customer document contents or personal data; engineers who find such content in logs report it to the security team and treat it as a security issue.

### 10.2 Metrics and dashboards

The standard dashboard shows request rate, error rate, latency percentiles, queue depth, worker count, and replica lag. The reliability team reviews the dashboard in a weekly operations meeting.

### 10.3 Audit logs

Audit logs record retrieval, prompt construction, model response, and user export events, as required by the security review. Audit logs are append-only and are not writable by the services that produce them.

## 11. Capacity planning

Every quarter the reliability team compares the volume of the last quarter with the platform's capacity. The rule of thumb is that the peak queue depth and the peak worker count should leave at least 30 percent headroom below the maximum of 24 workers. If the headroom falls below that, the team proposes an increase in the maximum in the next planning cycle.

## 12. Runbook maintenance

This runbook is reviewed after every Severity 1 incident and at least once per quarter. The reliability engineering lead owns it. Changes are proposed through the normal review process, and every change states which incident or finding prompted it.

## 13. Appendix A: alert reference

| Alert | Condition | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extraction queue warning | depth above 200 for more than 10 minutes | create a ticket |
| Extraction queue page | depth above 500 for 5 minutes | page on-call |
| Stuck worker | one message held for more than 10 minutes | supervisor restarts the worker |
| Replica lag | above 30 seconds | page on-call |
| Certificate expiry | 45, 14, and 3 days before expiry | ticket at 45 and 14 days, page at 3 days |
| Error rate | above 2 percent for 5 minutes | page on-call |
| Backup failure | nightly backup does not finish by 04:00 UTC | create a ticket and notify the reliability lead |
| Token rotation overdue | a service token older than 90 days | ticket to the service owner |

Every alert has an owner and a linked procedure. Alerts without either are removed at the quarterly review, because an alert that nobody knows how to handle is noise.

## 14. Appendix B: procedures

### 14.1 Scaling the extraction pool by hand

The autoscaler normally does this, but an engineer may override it during an incident. Set the desired worker count in the pool configuration and confirm in the dashboard that workers are joining. Do not exceed the maximum of 24 workers without the reliability lead's approval. Record the override in the incident timeline, and remove it afterward so that the autoscaler resumes control; a forgotten override is a common cause of surprise bills and surprise outages alike.

### 14.2 Draining a worker

To drain a worker, mark it as draining in the pool manager. It finishes its current message and takes no more. Once idle it can be stopped safely. Never stop a busy worker by killing its process, because the message it holds will not be returned to the queue until the visibility timeout of 10 minutes expires.

### 14.3 Restoring from backup

Identify the recovery point, which is either the latest nightly backup or a point in time using the transaction logs. Restore into an isolated environment first, never directly over the primary. Validate row counts and run the integrity checks. Only after validation, and with the incident commander's approval, promote the restored database. Record every step and its time, because the restore time is a measured value that feeds the recovery time objective.

### 14.4 Rolling back a configuration change

Configuration is versioned. To roll back, revert to the previous version in the configuration pipeline and run validation. For ClaimPilot routing rules this means running the routing validation step, which is mandatory. Routing changes take effect within 5 minutes of deployment, so confirm the outcome after that interval.

### 14.5 Rotating a leaked token

Revoke the token first and rotate second. Inform the security team and the owner of the affected tenant. Review the audit logs for the period of possible exposure and record the findings in the incident document.

## 15. Appendix C: lessons from 2024 incidents

The four Severity 1 incidents of 2024 each changed this runbook. The incident on 2024-02-14 raised the baseline extraction pool from 4 to 10 workers. The incident on 2024-04-12 added the 45-day certificate alert. The incident on 2024-06-30 made routing validation mandatory before any routing change is deployed. The incident on 2024-09-19 tightened the replica lag alert to 30 seconds and added an approval step before promoting a lagging replica.

A pattern runs through them: in each case a safeguard existed in principle but was too weak, too narrow, or too easy to skip. The runbook's rule of thumb is to treat every safeguard that depends on a person remembering to do something as a candidate for automation.

## 16. Appendix D: communication templates

### 16.1 First status page update

"We are investigating elevated errors affecting [product]. Customers may be unable to [symptom]. We will post another update within 30 minutes."

### 16.2 Update while investigating

"We have identified [area] as the likely source of the problem and are working on a fix. Some customers may still see [symptom]. Next update within 30 minutes."

### 16.3 Resolution

"The issue has been resolved as of [time] UTC. Service is operating normally. A post-incident report will follow within 5 business days."

Keep updates factual. Do not speculate about the cause until it is confirmed, and never name a customer in a public update.

## 17. Appendix E: glossary

**Baseline:** the minimum number of extraction workers that always run.
**Canary:** a small group of servers that receives a new version first.
**Dead-letter queue:** the holding place for messages that repeatedly fail.
**RPO:** recovery point objective, the maximum tolerable data loss measured in time.
**RTO:** recovery time objective, the maximum tolerable time to restore service.
**Visibility timeout:** how long a message stays hidden from other workers after one worker takes it.
