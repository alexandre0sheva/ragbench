# Claims Handling Standards

These standards describe how claims teams at RAGBench Mutual customers and at RAGBench Mutual's own managed-claims service handle a loss from notice to closure when ClaimPilot is used for triage. They set minimum expectations for speed, documentation, authority, and fairness. They are written for adjusters, senior adjusters, and claims managers.

## 1. Principles

Claims handling rests on four principles. Treat the customer fairly. Pay what is owed, promptly. Document every decision so that someone who was not there can understand it. Never let a tool, however good, substitute for your own judgment about a particular claim.

ClaimPilot is a triage assistant. It summarizes loss notices, classifies severity, and recommends a handling queue and an evidence checklist. It does not make payment decisions, and it does not decide coverage. Every recommendation it makes is advisory.

## 2. From notice to first contact

### 2.1 Receiving a notice

A loss notice arrives through the portal, email, or a telephone call logged by the contact center. ClaimPilot reads the notice and produces a summary, a severity label, and a recommended queue. The severity labels are low, medium, high, and critical.

### 2.2 Verifying the summary

Before contacting the insured, the adjuster must verify the ClaimPilot summary against the original loss notice. If the summary and the notice disagree, the notice wins, and the adjuster records the discrepancy so that it can be reported to the product team.

### 2.3 First contact targets

The insured must be contacted within the following times from the moment the notice is received:

| Severity | Maximum time to first contact |
| --- | --- |
| Low | 3 business days |
| Medium | 2 business days |
| High | 24 hours |
| Critical | 4 hours |

For a critical claim the contact must be a telephone call by a senior adjuster; an email alone does not satisfy the standard.

### 2.4 What makes a claim critical

A claim is critical if it involves a possible fatality, major litigation exposure, or a regulatory reporting deadline. When in doubt, treat the claim as critical and downgrade it later; it is much worse to under-escalate than to over-escalate.

## 3. Queues

ClaimPilot recommends one of five queues.

**FNOL-Fast.** Low-severity claims with simple facts. Target first touch is 15 minutes from the time the notice enters the queue.

**Standard.** Medium-severity claims. Target first touch is 60 minutes.

**Complex.** High-severity claims and any claim involving multiple parties. Target first touch is 120 minutes.

**Litigation.** Claims in which an attorney is involved, a lawsuit has been filed, or a subpoena has been received. Target first touch is 30 minutes, and the claim is assigned to a senior adjuster.

**SIU.** Claims with indicators of possible fraud. A claim with three or more red flags goes to the Special Investigations Unit. The adjuster must not tell the insured that the claim has been referred to the SIU.

### 3.1 Moving a claim between queues

Adjusters may move a claim to a different queue with a recorded reason. A claim moved from a lower to a higher severity queue restarts the first-contact clock. A claim must never be moved to a lower severity queue solely to meet a target.

## 4. Investigation and evidence

### 4.1 The evidence checklist

ClaimPilot generates an evidence checklist tailored to the loss type. The adjuster works through the checklist and records what was received, what is outstanding, and what is not applicable. Items marked not applicable need a short reason.

### 4.2 Statements

Statements from the insured and from witnesses are recorded in writing or by audio, with consent. The statement must be added to the file within 2 business days of being taken.

### 4.3 Experts

Instruct an expert such as a loss adjuster, engineer, or forensic accountant only when the facts cannot be established without one. Record why the expert was needed and what question they were asked to answer.

### 4.4 Preserving evidence

If physical evidence is involved, the adjuster must arrange for it to be preserved and must log who has custody of it. Evidence is never discarded while a claim is open or while a limitation period may still be running.

## 5. Reserves and payment authority

### 5.1 Setting reserves

A reserve is the estimate of what the claim will cost. The adjuster sets the initial reserve within 5 business days of the first contact and updates it whenever the facts change materially. Reserves are set on the most likely outcome, not the best or the worst case.

### 5.2 Authority limits

Payment and reserve authority is tiered:

- An adjuster may settle and reserve up to $15,000.
- A senior adjuster may settle and reserve up to $75,000.
- A claims manager may settle and reserve up to $250,000.
- Anything above $250,000 must go to the Claims Committee.

### 5.3 Authority for litigation claims

For claims in the litigation queue, settlement authority is one tier lower than shown above unless the file records an explicit instruction from the Claims Committee. In practice this means that a senior adjuster handling a litigation claim may settle only up to the adjuster limit unless authority has been extended.

### 5.4 Payments

Payments are made only after the required documents are on file. The person who approves a payment must not be the person who set up the payee. The payment run is reviewed weekly for duplicates, which are one of the main causes of leakage.

### 5.5 Partial and interim payments

Where liability is clear but the full amount is not yet known, the adjuster should offer an interim payment rather than waiting for the final figure. Interim payments are recorded against the reserve and count toward the authority limit.

## 6. Communication

### 6.1 Tone and clarity

Write to the insured in plain language. Avoid insurance jargon, and never use the tool's output as a script. Explain the next step and the expected timescale in every letter.

### 6.2 Updates

The insured must receive an update at least every 10 business days while the claim is open, even if the update is only that nothing has changed.

### 6.3 Acknowledgement letters

Acknowledgement letters may be generated automatically; this is the only step in the process that can complete without a human touch. Everything else requires an adjuster.

### 6.4 Language support

From ClaimPilot 2.4 a Spanish-language adjuster note template is available. Adjusters using it should still review the generated text for accuracy before it is sent.

## 7. Subrogation and recoveries

### 7.1 Identifying recovery opportunities

At first contact, and again whenever new facts emerge, the adjuster considers whether a third party might be responsible. If so, a recovery file is opened and the third party is put on notice promptly.

### 7.2 Time limits

Recovery actions are subject to limitation periods that vary by jurisdiction. The adjuster records the earliest limitation date on the file and sets a reminder 90 days before it.

### 7.3 Salvage

Where damaged property has residual value, the adjuster arranges for salvage to be sold at the best price reasonably available and records the proceeds against the claim.

## 8. Special situations

### 8.1 Claims involving injury

Claims involving injury are always handled by a senior adjuster. Medical records are requested with written consent, and only the records relevant to the claim are kept.

### 8.2 Catastrophe events

When a catastrophe event is declared, the cat desk sets up a temporary intake team and relaxes the standard first-contact targets to the times published in the catastrophe plan. The relaxed targets apply only while the event is declared.

### 8.3 Vulnerable customers

If the insured may be vulnerable because of age, health, bereavement, or another circumstance, the adjuster must adapt the approach, offer extra support, and note the adaptation on the file.

## 9. Quality and audit

### 9.1 File review

Each month a sample of 5 percent of closed files is reviewed by a claims manager who did not handle them. The review looks at timeliness, documentation, reserve accuracy, and the treatment of the insured.

### 9.2 Aged files

A file that has been open for more than 180 days is an aged file, known informally as a greybeard. Every aged file is reviewed at the monthly aged-file meeting and gets a documented plan for closure.

### 9.3 Leakage reviews

The finance and claims teams jointly review leakage each quarter, looking at duplicate payments, missed recoveries, and overpaid repairs. Findings are reported to the Claims Committee.

### 9.4 Reporting tool issues

If ClaimPilot produces a wrong severity label, a misleading summary, or a bad routing recommendation, report it through the feedback form with the notice reference and what the correct outcome should have been. Do not paste the customer's personal details into the free-text field.

## 10. Escalation and complaints

### 10.1 Complaints

A complaint is any expression of dissatisfaction, written or spoken. Complaints are logged within 1 business day and acknowledged within 2 business days. A claims manager investigates, and the insured receives a final response within 30 days.

### 10.2 Regulators

Where a regulator contacts the company about a claim, the adjuster informs the claims manager immediately and does not reply directly. Compliance coordinates the response.

### 10.3 Ending a claim

A claim is closed when payment is made, the claim is withdrawn, or the claim is denied with a clear written explanation. The file must contain a closure note summarizing the outcome and the reasons.

## 11. Appendix A: worked examples

### 11.1 A low-severity windscreen claim

A policyholder reports a cracked windscreen. ClaimPilot labels the notice low, recommends the FNOL-Fast queue, and produces an evidence checklist with photographs and the repair quote. The adjuster verifies the summary against the notice, contacts the insured within the 3 business day limit, approves the repair, and pays it within the adjuster's $15,000 authority. The file closes within a week.

### 11.2 A high-severity fire claim

A commercial fire is reported with significant damage and a possible business interruption loss. ClaimPilot labels it high and recommends the Complex queue. The adjuster calls the insured within 24 hours, the senior adjuster sets an initial reserve of $180,000, and because the reserve is above the senior adjuster limit of $75,000 the file is referred to a claims manager, whose authority reaches $250,000. As the facts develop the reserve rises to $310,000, which is above the claims manager limit, so the file moves to the Claims Committee.

### 11.3 A critical claim with a fatality

A notice reports a workplace accident with a possible fatality. ClaimPilot labels it critical. A senior adjuster telephones the insured within 4 hours. The adjuster notes regulatory reporting deadlines on the file and informs the claims manager at once. Because the facts may lead to litigation, the file is reviewed daily until the picture is clear.

### 11.4 A litigation claim with limited authority

An attorney writes on behalf of a claimant and threatens a lawsuit. ClaimPilot routes the claim to the Litigation queue, where first touch is targeted within 30 minutes. A senior adjuster is assigned. Under the litigation rule the senior adjuster's settlement authority is one tier lower, so the senior adjuster may settle only up to the adjuster limit of $15,000 unless the Claims Committee has extended authority in writing.

### 11.5 A suspected fraud referral

A notice reports a theft with inconsistent dates, a recently increased sum insured, and a missing police report number. The adjuster counts three red flags and refers the claim to the SIU. The adjuster does not tell the insured that the claim has been referred and continues to handle routine communications neutrally while the SIU investigates.

## 13. Appendix C: documentation standards

A good file note says what happened, what was decided, why, and what happens next. Notes are written at the time, not reconstructed later. Each note has a date, the author's name, and a clear heading. Use plain words, and never write opinions about the insured's character. Anything you would not want read aloud to the insured or a regulator does not belong in the file.

Reserve changes need a note explaining the reason, even when the change is small. Payments need a note that links the payment to the evidence. A closure note summarizes the claim in one paragraph and lists any open recovery actions.

When ClaimPilot output is used, say so in the note, and say whether you agreed with it. If you disagreed, record why. These notes are how the product team learns where the tool is weak.

## 14. Appendix D: working with the contact center

The contact center takes the first telephone notice of loss and enters it in the portal. Adjusters should treat the contact center's notes as a starting point rather than a verified record. If a detail in the notice is wrong, correct it and tell the contact center so that the script can be improved.

For critical claims the contact center alerts the senior adjuster on duty immediately, without waiting for ClaimPilot to process the notice. The senior adjuster confirms by return that the alert was received. If the confirmation does not arrive within 10 minutes, the contact center calls the claims manager.

## 15. Appendix E: fair treatment checklist

Before closing any file, the adjuster confirms that the insured understood the decision and the reasons for it, that any denial cites the policy wording, that the insured was told how to complain and to whom, that vulnerable-customer needs were considered, and that nothing in the file suggests that the insured was treated differently because of a protected characteristic. A file that fails this checklist is reopened by the claims manager.
