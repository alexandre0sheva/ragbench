# RAGBench Mutual Employee Handbook

Welcome to RAGBench Mutual. This handbook explains how we work together: what you can expect from the company and what the company expects from you. It is written in plain language on purpose. Where a topic has its own detailed policy, such as remote work, the handbook points to that policy instead of repeating it, so that there is only one place to keep up to date.

The handbook applies to all full-time and part-time employees. Contractors are covered by their contract and by the security and conduct sections of this handbook only.

## 1. Who we are and how we are organized

RAGBench Mutual builds decision-support tools for insurance carriers, brokers, and claims teams. Our three products are HarborShield AI, ClaimPilot, and Aurora Risk Suite. Around those products sit the teams that keep them running: Product, Engineering, Customer Operations, Compliance, Finance, and People.

Every employee has a manager, and every manager has a named director. Your manager is the first person to go to with questions about your work, your schedule, your growth, and anything in this handbook that is unclear. If you cannot resolve a question with your manager, the People team is the next stop. The organization directory lists who reports to whom for the product, engineering, and customer operations functions.

We use a small number of shared systems. Work tracking happens in the team boards, written decisions live in the wiki, and customer conversations live in the support desk. If something matters, it should be written down in one of those three places. A decision made only in a chat thread is a decision that will be remade next quarter.

## 2. Starting at the company

### The first week

On your first day you receive a laptop, a security badge, and access to the password manager. Your manager assigns a buddy from outside your immediate team. The buddy's job is to answer the questions that feel too small to ask your manager, such as where to find the good coffee and which meetings are optional.

### Probation and onboarding

The first 90 days are an onboarding period. You and your manager agree on three goals in the first two weeks and review them at 30, 60, and 90 days. The 90-day review is a conversation, not an exam; its purpose is to make sure you have what you need to succeed.

### Mandatory training

New employees must complete security awareness training within 14 days of their start date. Access to production systems and to customer data is not granted until the training is complete. Everyone repeats the training every 12 months. In addition, the security team runs a phishing simulation every quarter; employees who click a simulated phishing link are asked to repeat a short module but are never disciplined for it.

## 3. Working time and leave

### Working hours

We do not track hours for salaried employees. We do expect that core collaboration hours are respected: between 10:00 and 15:00 in your team's home time zone you should be reachable for meetings. Outside those hours you are free to organize your day.

Where you work is covered by the Remote Work Policy, which states how many days per week you are expected in the office, what home office support you receive, and the rules for working from abroad. Always check the current version of that policy; older versions remain on the wiki for reference but no longer apply.

### Paid time off

Full-time employees accrue paid time off at a rate of 1.75 days per month, which adds up to 21 days per year. Accrual begins on your start date and you may use accrued days as you earn them. You may carry over at most 5 unused days into the following year, and carried-over days must be used by 31 March of that year or they expire. Part-time employees accrue in proportion to their contracted hours.

To take time off, request it in the leave system at least one week ahead for absences of up to three days, and at least one month ahead for longer absences. Managers approve requests unless the team would be left without coverage; if a request is declined, the manager must propose alternative dates.

### Public holidays

The company observes the public holidays of the country where you are employed. The shared company calendar lists the holidays for each hub and the dates of the year-end change freeze.

### Sick leave

Each employee receives 10 paid sick days per year, separate from paid time off. A medical note is required after 3 consecutive sick days. Sick days do not carry over. If you are unwell, tell your manager as early as you can; you do not need to explain the illness.

### Parental leave

The primary caregiver receives 16 weeks of fully paid parental leave. The secondary caregiver receives 8 weeks of fully paid leave. Leave can be taken in one block or split into two blocks within the first 12 months after the birth or adoption. Health benefits continue unchanged during leave, and your role is held for you.

### Other paid leave

- **Bereavement:** 5 days for the loss of an immediate family member, and 3 days for extended family.
- **Jury duty:** paid for up to 10 days per year; submit the summons to your manager.
- **Volunteering:** 2 paid days per year to volunteer for a registered charity.
- **Moving home:** 1 paid day.

## 4. Compensation and benefits

### Pay and payroll

Salaries are paid monthly, on the last business day of the month. Pay is reviewed annually as part of the performance cycle described below. Any bonus or equity is described in your offer letter and is not covered by this handbook.

### Health and wellbeing

The company funds health, dental, and vision cover for you and your dependants. The employee assistance program offers free, confidential counselling sessions; you do not need your manager's approval to use it. A monthly wellbeing allowance can be spent on fitness, meditation apps, or similar; ask the People team for the current amount and rules.

### Pension

The company contributes to your pension or retirement plan in line with local law and your offer letter. The People team can explain the options in your country.

### Learning budget

Every employee has a personal learning budget of $1,200 per calendar year. It can be spent on books, courses, certifications, and conference tickets. Unused budget does not roll over to the next year. Conference travel is not covered by the learning budget; it needs separate manager approval under the travel rules below.

### Laptop and equipment

Laptops are refreshed every 36 months. If your laptop fails before then, the IT desk will repair or replace it within two business days. Peripherals such as a monitor, keyboard, or headset can be requested through the IT desk at any time.

### Referral bonus

If you refer a candidate who is hired and who completes 90 days of employment, you receive a referral bonus of $3,000. The bonus is paid with the payroll following the candidate's 90th day. Managers cannot receive a referral bonus for candidates in their own reporting line.

## 5. Expenses and travel

### What you can claim

The company reimburses reasonable business expenses. Submit your claim within 30 days of spending the money; claims older than 30 days need a note from your director. Receipts are required for any single expense over $25.

### Meals

When travelling on company business you receive a daily meal allowance: $65 per day for domestic trips and $85 per day for international trips. You do not need to keep receipts for meals within the allowance. Alcohol is not reimbursable.

### Accommodation and transport

Hotels are capped at $280 per night in major cities and $190 per night elsewhere. Book economy class for flights under six hours. For longer flights, premium economy is acceptable with manager approval. Use the train where the journey is under four hours and the price is comparable to flying.

### Approvals

Expenses and purchases are approved by level: your manager can approve up to $1,000, your director up to $5,000, and anything above $5,000 needs approval from the Chief Technology Officer or the head of Finance. Do not split a purchase into smaller pieces to stay under a limit; the finance team looks for this pattern and will ask you about it.

### Gifts and hospitality

You may accept or give gifts and hospitality worth up to $100 per year per source. Anything above that must be disclosed to Compliance before you accept it. Cash and cash equivalents may never be accepted. A customer or vendor who insists on giving you something larger should be pointed to Compliance.

## 6. Security and conduct

### Protecting customer data

Customer data is the most sensitive thing we handle. Do not copy customer data to personal devices, personal cloud accounts, or chat messages. Use the scoped service tokens issued to you and never share them. When you need production data to reproduce a bug, ask your team lead to arrange a masked extract.

### Accounts and devices

Use the company password manager for all work credentials. Multi-factor authentication is mandatory on every system that supports it. Lock your screen when you step away. Report a lost or stolen device to the IT desk immediately, at any hour; the faster we can wipe it, the less there is to worry about.

### Reporting a security concern

Email the security team or use the `#security-help` channel. You will never be criticised for reporting something that turns out to be harmless. You can also report anonymously through the ethics line.

### Code of conduct

We treat each other with respect. Harassment, discrimination, and bullying are not tolerated in any form, including in chat and at company events. If you experience or witness such behavior, tell your manager, the People team, or the ethics line. Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith is itself a serious breach of this code.

### Conflicts of interest

If you or a close family member has a financial interest in a competitor, customer, or vendor, disclose it to Compliance before you take part in any decision involving that organization. Outside work for another employer requires written approval from your director.

## 7. On-call and incident duty

Engineers on the reliability, platform, and product engineering teams take turns on the on-call rotation. A rotation lasts one week. On-call employees receive an on-call stipend of $350 per week. In addition, each after-hours page that takes more than 30 minutes of active work earns an extra $75.

The handoff between on-call engineers happens every Monday at 09:00 UTC, with a written handoff note in the reliability channel. If you are on call, keep your laptop and a charged phone within reach, and stay within a reasonable distance of reliable connectivity. If you will not be able to respond within 15 minutes, swap your shift in advance.

During a Severity 1 incident the incident commander has authority over the response, regardless of seniority. Engineers take direction from the commander until the commander declares the incident resolved. After every Severity 1 incident the incident owner writes a post-incident report within 5 business days.

## 8. Performance and growth

### Review cycle

Performance is reviewed twice a year, in May and in November. Each review combines a self-assessment, feedback from at least three colleagues, and your manager's assessment. The November review also feeds the annual pay review. You will always see your manager's written assessment before the conversation, so that there are no surprises.

### Promotions

Promotions are considered at the November review. The bar is demonstrated performance at the next level over a sustained period, not potential. Your manager will tell you candidly where you stand and what evidence would make the case stronger.

### Feedback

Good feedback is specific, kind, and timely. Do not save it up for the review. If you receive feedback you disagree with, say so; the conversation is more useful than silence.

## 9. Leaving the company

### Resignation

Please give notice in line with your contract. Your manager will agree a handover plan with you, and the People team will arrange an exit conversation that you are free to decline.

### Returning equipment and access

Return your laptop, badge, and any other equipment on or before your last day. Access to all systems, including the password manager and the support desk, is removed at the end of your last day. Do not keep copies of customer data under any circumstances.

### Confidentiality after you leave

Your confidentiality obligations continue after your employment ends. If you are unsure what you may share about your time at the company, ask Compliance before you post or publish anything.

## 10. Changes to this handbook

The People team owns this handbook and reviews it every year. Substantive changes are announced in the all-hands channel and dated in the wiki history. If you believe something in the handbook is out of date or unfair, tell your manager or the People team; corrections are welcome and usually fast.

## Appendix A. Booking travel step by step

1. Get approval from your manager for the trip and its purpose before you book anything. Approval by chat is fine as long as it is written.
2. Book through the approved travel portal, which applies the fare and hotel caps automatically. Flights should be booked at least 14 days ahead; last-minute fares above the cap need director approval.
3. Choose compact or midsize cars for rentals. Upgrades are at your own expense unless you are carrying equipment that does not fit.
4. Use the company card for travel bookings where possible, so that there is nothing to claim back afterward.
5. Keep your boarding passes and hotel folio. The folio is the receipt for accommodation and is always required, even if the nightly rate was under the receipt threshold.
6. If you drive your own car for business, mileage is reimbursed at $0.67 per mile. Commuting is never reimbursed.
7. After the trip, submit the expense report within the 30-day window.

Travelling customers' staff or guests are not covered by your allowance. If you host a customer, record their names and the business purpose on the expense claim.

## Appendix B. Internal mobility and secondments

You may apply for an internal role after 12 months in your current role. Tell your manager at the same time you apply; nobody should learn about your application from the hiring manager. The hiring manager must not make an offer without speaking to your current manager.

A secondment to another team can last up to 6 months. Your pay and benefits stay the same, and your home team keeps your position open. A secondment can be extended once, for up to another 3 months, if both managers and the director agree.

After 5 years of service, employees may take a paid sabbatical of 4 weeks. The sabbatical must be scheduled with your manager at least three months ahead and cannot be combined with more than one week of regular paid time off. Service awards are given at 3, 5, and 10 years.

## Appendix C. Workplace rules

Visitors must be signed in at reception, given a visitor badge, and accompanied at all times. Do not let a stranger follow you through a secured door; politely ask them to use reception. Wear your badge visibly in the office.

The quiet room on the fourth floor is for focus work and is not for phone calls. Bicycles can be stored in the basement rack. Food in meeting rooms is fine as long as you clear it afterward. Whiteboards in meeting rooms are wiped nightly, so photograph anything you need and do not use them to store customer information.

If you notice a safety hazard, tell the facilities team immediately. Fire drills happen twice a year, and everyone takes part.

## Appendix D. Speaking for the company

Only the Communications team makes statements on behalf of the company. If a journalist contacts you, do not comment; forward the enquiry to Communications within 1 business day. On social media you are welcome to share your own professional views, but make clear they are your own, and never disclose customer names, customer data, or unreleased product plans. Customer names may be mentioned publicly only with written permission from Customer Operations.

Do not use customer data, even anonymized, in talks or blog posts without the approval of Compliance. Conference talks need approval from your director at least two weeks before the submission deadline.

## Appendix E. Bribery, corruption, and facilitation

The company has zero tolerance for bribery. You must not offer, promise, give, request, or accept anything of value to influence a business decision. Facilitation payments, meaning small payments to speed up routine official actions, are prohibited even where local practice treats them as normal.

If someone offers you a bribe or asks you for one, refuse, and report it to Compliance within 24 hours. Compliance records every report and keeps the reporter's identity confidential. Third parties who act for us, such as agents and resellers, are contractually bound by the same rules.

## Appendix F. Frequently asked questions

**Can I bring my own laptop?** No. Only company-issued laptops may access customer data, in line with the Remote Work Policy.

**What if I lose my badge?** Tell reception the same day. A replacement badge takes one business day, and the lost badge is deactivated immediately.

**How soon must I report a suspected data breach?** Report it to the security team within 1 hour of noticing it, even if you are not sure that it is a breach. The security team decides whether it is one.

**Can I use generative AI tools at work?** Only tools approved by the security team. Never paste customer data, source citations, or unreleased product plans into an unapproved tool.

**Who do I call if I feel unsafe?** Emergency services first, then the People team. The ethics line is available at any hour and is staffed by an independent provider.

**How do I change my bank details?** Through the payroll portal, with multi-factor authentication. The People team will never ask you to send bank details by email.
