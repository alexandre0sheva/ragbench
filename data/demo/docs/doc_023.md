# Underwriting and Claims Slang: An Insider's Glossary

Maintained by the Customer Operations team for new joiners. This is how people actually talk on the floor, not how the policy documents talk. If a customer says something you do not recognize, start here.

## Portfolio and market talk

**The book.** A carrier's whole collection of in-force policies. "How does the book look in the Gulf?" means how the portfolio is performing in that region.

**Hard market / soft market.** In a hard market premiums climb and capacity dries up; underwriters can pick and choose. In a soft market capacity is plentiful and prices sag.

**Cede.** To hand part of a risk (and some of the premium) to a reinsurer. A carrier that cedes a lot is "heavily ceded".

**Nat-cat.** Natural catastrophe exposure: hurricanes, floods, quakes. The "cat desk" is the small team that models it.

## Submission talk

**Clean packet.** A broker submission that arrives with all nine required intake fields filled in. A packet with gaps is a "dirty packet".

**Bounce.** Sending a submission back to the broker because something essential is missing. A bounce costs the broker a day, so underwriters try to avoid them on clean-ish packets.

**Sitting on a file.** Leaving a submission untouched for longer than the service target. Nobody admits to doing it; everybody has done it.

**Bordereau.** A spreadsheet a broker sends, usually monthly, listing every risk or claim handled under a delegated-authority arrangement. Pronounced "bord-er-oh".

## Claims talk

**Touch.** One human handling of a claim. "First-touch time" is how long a claim waits between the loss notice arriving and a person looking at it for the first time.

**Leakage.** Money paid out on claims beyond what a correct process would have paid: duplicate payments, missed subrogation, overpaid repairs. Leakage is the enemy; ClaimPilot exists partly to shrink it.

**Zero-touch.** A step completed with no human involved. At RAGBench Mutual this only ever applies to acknowledgement letters; ClaimPilot never decides payments, so no claim is ever zero-touch end to end.

**Tail.** The slow, long-running end of a claims cohort, typically injury or litigation claims that keep developing for years.

**Greybeard.** A claim file that has been open for more than 180 days. Claims managers review every greybeard at the monthly aged-file meeting.

**Red-flag file.** A claim with indicators of possible fraud. Three red flags send it to the Special Investigations Unit (SIU).
